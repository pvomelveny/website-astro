Why is the matching polytope half-integral?

A look at what LP relaxations know about matchings, and why half-integrality is the best we can hope for without additional constraints.

Given a graph , the matching polytope is the convex hull of all incidence vectors of matchings in . Understanding its facet structure is central to both combinatorial optimization and polyhedral combinatorics.1

The natural LP relaxation of the maximum weight matching problem is the fractional matching polytope, defined by

It is a classical result that every vertex of this polytope is half-integral — every coordinate takes a value in .2 This is perhaps surprising: why should the LP know to use only these values?

The structure of half-integral solutions

A half-integral solution decomposes naturally into three edge sets: those with , those with , and those with . The edges with value form a union of odd cycles — this is the key structural insight.3

Edmonds' characterization adds the odd-set constraints that cut off exactly these half-integral vertices, yielding a TDI system whose integer hull is the matching polytope.
